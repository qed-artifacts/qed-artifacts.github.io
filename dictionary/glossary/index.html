evaluative basis or consideration

Criterion

A criterion is a consideration used as a basis for a judgment.

Read explanation

In evaluation, it identifies something that matters about the subject being assessed, such as accessibility, safety, relevance, or timeliness.

The evaluator must still establish why that consideration matters and what evidence can support a judgment about it.

The subject being assessed is an evaluand; criteria provide grounds for judging its merit or worth.

evaluation terminology

Evaluand

An evaluand is the specific thing being evaluated.

Read explanation

Depending on the evaluation, it may be a program, policy, product, service, organization, person’s performance, or other subject.

The term names a role in an evaluation: it does not, by itself, say whether that thing is good, effective, or valuable.

cross-domain elevated appraisal

Excellence

Excellence is exceptionally strong merit or performance in a relevant respect, judged by standards appropriate to the activity, object, or person.

Read explanation

Calling something excellent therefore requires more than saying that it passed a minimum requirement: the judgment must identify what it excels at and provide a sound basis for the elevated appraisal.

cross-domain evaluative sense

Goodness

Goodness is favorable evaluative standing.

Read explanation

To understand a particular claim of goodness, ask: What is good, in what respect, for whom if anyone, and by which standards?

This is a usable cross-domain definition, rather than a claim that philosophy has settled on one analysis of every use of good.

The logical status of “good” remains disputed.

evaluation and monitoring

Indicator

An indicator is an observable or assessable factor used to track something of interest.

Read explanation

It helps answer an evaluation question, but its value requires interpretation: even an accurately assessed indicator is an approximation of the condition or change of interest.

Indicators can be qualitative or quantitative.

quality management

Quality

Quality is how well an object’s relevant characteristics fulfill the requirements used to judge it.

Read explanation

Here, relevant characteristics are inherent characteristics of the object that bear on the applicable requirements. An attribute merely assigned to the object is outside this definition of quality.

In quality management, object can mean a product, service, process, organization, or other subject of assessment.

Requirements may be stated, generally implied, or obligatory; they are not limited to written specifications.

quality assessment across domains

Quality indicator

A quality indicator is a specified sign used to gather evidence about one aspect of quality.

Read explanation

It can describe a feature of an object, how a service is provided, or a result experienced by its users.

Its interpretation depends on the quality question, the population and setting, the data, and the relationship between the sign and the aspect being judged.

In health care, quality indicators may concern structure, process, or outcome.

cross-domain evaluative conclusion

Quality judgment

A quality judgment is a reasoned conclusion about how well an object meets relevant requirements or evaluative criteria.

Read explanation

It identifies the object and the basis for judging it, interprets pertinent evidence, and states the resulting appraisal.

The conclusion may be qualified when evidence is incomplete or criteria conflict.

evaluative reference; documentary and measurement uses distinguished

Standard

In an evaluative use, a standard is a reference or expected level used to judge performance or acceptability in a stated context.

Read explanation

A criterion identifies a consideration for judgment, while a standard can specify the level or condition expected for it; the terms overlap in some fields.

An indicator supplies an observable or assessable sign relevant to the judgment, but its result alone does not establish that the chosen standard is appropriate.

A standard can be communicated through descriptions, exemplars, or numerical limits; a defensible judgment names whose standard applies and what decision it governs.

The word also has other uses: a documentary standard may specify terminology, procedures, tests, or requirements, while a measurement standard is a reference for quantity values.

evaluative sense

Value

In its evaluative sense, value is the significance something has under an account of what merits concern, appreciation, use, or pursuit.

Read explanation

A useful value claim says what matters, to whom, in what setting, and why.

Evaluation research distinguishes factual description from a judgment that brings evidence and relevant values together.

broad evaluative and practical-decision use

Worth

In its broad evaluative use, worth is the value, merit, or importance attributed to something on stated grounds.

Read explanation

In a practical decision, “Is it worth it?” asks whether the reasons to pursue or obtain something justify its effort, cost, risk, and forgone alternatives.

That question may call for judgment without yielding a single monetary answer.
