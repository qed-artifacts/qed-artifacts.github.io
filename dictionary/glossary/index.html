Glossary of Quality

specified acceptance decision

Acceptance criterion

An acceptance criterion is a condition or decision rule used to determine whether a specified item, result, or process may be accepted for a stated purpose.

Read explanation

It can specify a limit, an observable condition, or another checkable basis for the decision. State what is being accepted, how the condition is checked, and who may decide.

A requirement may need one or more acceptance criteria to make its fulfillment assessable. Passing one criterion does not by itself establish the object’s overall quality.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

purpose-bound examination

Assessment

Assessment is the process of gathering and interpreting information about a specified subject for a stated question or decision.

Read explanation

An assessment may compare evidence with requirements, criteria, or a reference, then report findings or a judgment. Its methods and evidential demands depend on its purpose.

The term is used differently across fields. It often overlaps with evaluation; an assessment need not always conclude with an overall appraisal of merit or worth.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

descriptive property of an object

Attribute

An attribute is a feature or property ascribed to, observed in, or recorded for an object.

Read explanation

An attribute can be described by a category, a number, or words. Its recorded state is different from the attribute itself.

The label alone does not show that the feature is inherent, quality-relevant, or favorable. State the object and the role the attribute plays in the assessment.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evaluation comparison reference

Benchmark

A benchmark is a reference for comparison.

Read explanation

In evaluation, it helps answer “How does this result compare with what a relevant peer has achieved, or with what appears achievable under similar conditions?”

A relevant comparison may use recent performance by similar organizations or a reasoned estimate of what is achievable under similar conditions.

In some uses, a benchmark specifically represents leading practice or performance.

Thus, a benchmark need not mean “the best” in every field.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

descriptive feature of an object

Characteristic

A characteristic is a distinguishable feature by which an object can be described.

Read explanation

It may be observed directly, inferred, or assigned in a description. Naming a characteristic does not say whether it is desirable.

To use one in a quality judgment, identify the object and explain how that feature bears on an applicable requirement or other stated basis.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

meeting stated requirements

Conformance

Conformance is the state or degree to which an object or activity meets applicable requirements or a stated specification.

Read explanation

A conformance claim needs to identify the object, the requirements checked, and the evidence used. Meeting a specification establishes only what that specification covers; it may leave a user’s other needs unaddressed.

Conformance and conformity are often used interchangeably. Neither word, by itself, names a distinct kind of quality.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

fulfillment of an applicable requirement

Conformity

Conformity is the condition of meeting an applicable requirement.

Read explanation

A claim of conformity should say which object and requirement are involved. When several requirements apply, identify the set covered by the claim and how fulfillment was established.

Conformity to a requirement does not guarantee suitability for every use. In many contexts, conformance names the same relationship.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

addressing causes of a detected nonconformity

Corrective action

Corrective action addresses the cause of a detected nonconformity or other unwanted situation so it does not recur.

Read explanation

Repairing or replacing one affected item corrects that case. Corrective action changes the condition that produced it; the two may both be needed.

The action should follow an investigation proportionate to the problem. Its effectiveness must be checked against later evidence rather than assumed from implementation alone.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

CAPA approach to actual and potential quality problems

Corrective and preventive action

Corrective and preventive action, or CAPA, is a structured approach to addressing actual and potential quality problems through the actions appropriate to each case.

Read explanation

It distinguishes correcting a detected case, addressing a cause of recurrence, and addressing a cause of a potential problem. These are different tasks; a case does not automatically require every kind of action.

A CAPA record can connect investigation, planned actions, implementation, and an effectiveness check. Its purpose is to show that the response fits the problem and that the intended change occurred.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

technical-interaction roof in a House of Quality

Correlation matrix

In a House of Quality, the correlation matrix is a roof-shaped grid for assessing how proposed technical characteristics interact with one another.

Read explanation

A positive mark suggests that changes may reinforce one another; a negative mark flags a possible conflict or trade-off. The meaning of each mark depends on the method and evidence used.

In this use, “correlation” need not mean a calculated statistical correlation coefficient. The roof records design judgments unless a separate analysis establishes measured relationships.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evaluative basis or consideration

Criterion

A criterion is a consideration used as a basis for a judgment.

Read explanation

In evaluation, it identifies something that matters about the subject being assessed, such as accessibility, safety, relevance, or timeliness.

The evaluator must still establish why that consideration matters and what evidence can support a judgment about it.

The subject being assessed is an evaluand; criteria provide grounds for judging its merit or worth.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

customer-side need or expectation in QFD

Customer requirement

In QFD, a customer requirement is a stated or investigated need or expectation that a proposed product or service should satisfy for a specified customer and use.

Read explanation

A broad request may need to be clarified into terms that can guide design and later assessment. An inferred need should be labeled and checked rather than presented as something the customer explicitly said.

The requirement describes the customer-side aim. A technical characteristic is a proposed design response, and a technical target specifies a desired level for that response.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

choice of a course of action

Decision

A decision is a choice among possible courses of action, including whether to act, defer, or seek more information.

Read explanation

A defensible decision identifies the options, the aim, the relevant evidence, and the likely consequences and uncertainties. A judgment or evaluation can inform the choice without uniquely determining it.

The decision is the choice made; its supporting inference, criteria, and evidence are distinct parts of its justification.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

nonconformity related to intended or specified use

Defect

In quality management, a defect is a nonconformity related to an intended or specified use.

Read explanation

The link to use matters: a departure may violate a requirement without being shown to affect the use in question. Identify the object, the unmet requirement, and the use before applying the narrower label.

A defect may be found through inspection, testing, or use. Finding one calls for a response to the affected case; preventing recurrence may also require corrective action.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

aspect in an evaluative framework

Dimension

In an evaluative framework, a dimension is a distinguishable aspect used to organize what is described or judged.

Read explanation

For data quality, accuracy and completeness can be treated as different dimensions. Which dimensions matter depends on the subject and purpose.

A dimension names an aspect; an indicator or measure supplies information about it. This use differs from mathematical dimensions such as length or time.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evaluation terminology

Evaluand

An evaluand is the specific thing being evaluated.

Read explanation

Depending on the evaluation, it may be a program, policy, product, service, organization, person’s performance, or other subject.

The term names a role in an evaluation: it does not, by itself, say whether that thing is good, effective, or valuable.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

reasoned appraisal of a subject

Evaluation

Evaluation is the process of appraising a specified subject against relevant grounds to reach a conclusion about its merit, worth, significance, or other value.

Read explanation

It brings information together with criteria or values and interprets the result for a stated purpose. The word can also name the resulting appraisal.

An evaluation may draw on assessments, measurements, or testimony; none of those alone settles the value judgment. What counts as adequate evidence depends on the question and setting.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

cross-domain claim-bearing evidence

Evidence

Evidence is information or material brought to bear on a claim: for example, observations, records, testimony, or a physical object.

Read explanation

What it establishes depends on which claim is at issue and how the material was obtained and interpreted.

Evidence can support a claim, count against it, or be too weak to settle it.

There is no single evidence threshold that applies across research, quality audits, and courts.

In quality management, objective evidence is data that supports the existence or truth of something. In U.S. federal evidence law, relevance depends on whether material makes a consequential fact more or less probable.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

cross-domain elevated appraisal

Excellence

Excellence is exceptionally strong merit or performance in a relevant respect, judged by standards appropriate to the activity, object, or person.

Read explanation

Calling something excellent therefore requires more than saying that it passed a minimum requirement: the judgment must identify what it excels at and provide a sound basis for the elevated appraisal.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

suitability for a stated aim

Fitness for purpose

Fitness for purpose is how suitable an object is to achieve a specified purpose under relevant conditions.

Read explanation

State the purpose, whose purpose it is, and the conditions that matter. A claim may concern an ordinary intended use or a specially declared one.

The phrase often overlaps with fitness for use. It does not prove that every requirement is met, and meeting listed requirements does not always establish fitness for the intended purpose.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

suitability in a user’s context

Fitness for use

Fitness for use is how suitable an object is for a user’s intended activity under the conditions in which it will be used.

Read explanation

The judgment depends on the user, task, setting, and consequences of failure. An object can meet its written specification yet be unsuitable for the intended use if the specification missed a relevant need.

Fitness for use and fitness for purpose overlap. This wording emphasizes the conditions of actual or intended use, without assigning the phrases mutually exclusive meanings.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

cross-domain evaluative sense

Goodness

Goodness is favorable evaluative standing.

Read explanation

To understand a particular claim of goodness, ask: What is good, in what respect, for whom if anyone, and by which standards?

This is a usable cross-domain definition, rather than a claim that philosophy has settled on one analysis of every use of good.

The logical status of “good” remains disputed.

customer-to-design planning matrix used in QFD

House of Quality

A House of Quality is a planning matrix that maps customer requirements to proposed product or service characteristics, recording how those characteristics could address the requirements to guide design priorities and targets.

Read explanation

Its central grid contains assessed need-to-response relationships. Depending on the method, it may also show customer importance, interactions among characteristics, comparisons with alternatives, and technical targets.

The matrix supports design decisions within QFD. Its scores and targets are planning judgments, not evidence that the finished offering performs as intended.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evaluation and monitoring

Indicator

An indicator is an observable or assessable factor used to track something of interest.

Read explanation

It helps answer an evaluation question, but its value requires interpretation: even an accurately assessed indicator is an approximation of the condition or change of interest.

Indicators can be qualitative or quantitative.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

reasoning from grounds to a conclusion

Inference

An inference is a move in reasoning from premises, observations, or other information to a further conclusion.

Read explanation

The relation between the grounds and conclusion may be deductive, statistical, causal, or another form. The conclusion is warranted only to the extent that the grounds and the inferential connection support it.

An inference can be made explicit so others can test its assumptions and limits. It is distinct from the observations that start it and the decision that may follow.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

determining conformity to specified requirements

Inspection

Inspection is an examination used to determine whether an object meets specified requirements.

Read explanation

The object, requirements, and inspection method determine what the result establishes. A passing sample does not by itself establish that every item or future output will conform.

Inspection can supply evidence for quality control or assurance. It does not by itself settle whether the requirements are suitable for the object’s intended use.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

conclusion drawn from information

Judgment

A judgment is a conclusion reached about a matter by interpreting information in light of a relevant basis for deciding.

Read explanation

The basis may be a rule, criterion, comparison, or informed professional appraisal. A useful judgment makes that basis and any important uncertainty visible.

Judgments may be factual or evaluative. A quality judgment is the narrower case that appraises how well an object meets relevant quality requirements or criteria.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

program evaluation evidence specification

Measure

In program evaluation, a measure is a specified observation or calculation used to produce evidence about an indicator.

Read explanation

It tells an evaluator what to record or compute.

A measure may be quantitative or qualitative; an indicator identifies what the measure bears on.

In metrology, measurement names the process, while measurement result names its output; measure is therefore too ambiguous to substitute for either term in a technical record.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

failure to fulfill an applicable requirement

Nonconformity

A nonconformity is a failure to fulfill an applicable requirement.

Read explanation

A useful finding identifies the object, the requirement, and the evidence of the departure. It should not treat a preference or an unstated expectation as a violated requirement without establishing its status.

A nonconformity may call for correction of the current case, investigation of a cause, or both. It is a defect in the narrower technical sense when the unmet requirement relates to an intended or specified use.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

addressing causes of a potential nonconformity

Preventive action

Preventive action addresses a cause of a potential nonconformity or other unwanted situation before it occurs.

Read explanation

It responds to an identified risk, whereas corrective action responds to a detected problem and seeks to prevent recurrence. The proposed action should match the plausible cause and the consequence at stake.

Some management systems incorporate prevention into risk-based planning rather than a separately named preventive-action step. The label does not, by itself, show that a risk was identified or reduced.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

quality management

Quality

Quality is how well an object’s relevant characteristics fulfill the requirements used to judge it.

Read explanation

Here, relevant characteristics are inherent characteristics of the object that bear on the applicable requirements. An attribute merely assigned to the object is outside this definition of quality.

In quality management, object can mean a product, service, process, organization, or other subject of assessment.

Requirements may be stated, generally implied, or obligatory; they are not limited to written specifications.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

examination of quality against a stated basis

Quality assessment

Quality assessment is the examination of evidence about an object’s quality against relevant requirements or criteria.

Read explanation

It may examine characteristics, performance, processes, or outcomes. State what was examined, the basis of comparison, and the limits of the evidence.

An assessment can report findings that support a later quality judgment, or include that judgment itself. The precise boundary between quality assessment and quality evaluation depends on the field and task.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

confidence in future fulfillment of quality requirements

Quality assurance

Quality assurance is the part of quality management concerned with providing a sound basis for expecting quality requirements to be fulfilled.

Read explanation

Planning, assigned responsibilities, checks, and evidence can support that expectation. Earlier results and present checks matter when they bear on future fulfillment; no assurance activity guarantees every result.

A check may serve both assurance and control. Assurance emphasizes warranted confidence in what will happen; quality control emphasizes work that fulfills the requirements.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

quality-model feature

Quality attribute

A quality attribute is a named feature of an object treated as relevant to an account of its quality.

Read explanation

In systems and software work, a model may organize attributes such as reliability or performance efficiency into narrower attributes and connect them to measures.

Naming an attribute does not give its measured value or decide whether performance is good. The term overlaps with quality characteristic in some fields; specify the model and sense being used.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

systematic quality-related audit against stated criteria

Quality audit

A quality audit is a systematic, documented, and independent assessment of a quality-related product, process, or system against stated audit criteria.

Read explanation

It uses relevant, verifiable evidence to determine where the criteria are met and where findings require attention. The scope and criteria must be named before a finding can be interpreted.

An audit may examine a product, a process, or a management system. Inspection of an output can contribute evidence, but an audit also considers the wider questions set by its scope.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

pharmaceutical product and process development

Quality by design

In pharmaceutical development, quality by design is a systematic approach that starts with intended product quality and uses product and process understanding to build and control that quality.

Read explanation

It sets a target product profile, identifies critical quality attributes, and studies the material and process variables that affect them. Those relationships guide development and the controls needed to produce the intended result.

Testing a finished product can check what was produced; quality by design also asks how development and manufacturing choices make the desired quality likely and controllable. Uses of the phrase outside pharmaceuticals require their own scope and evidence.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

quality management

Quality characteristic

A quality characteristic is an inherent feature of an object that bears on an applicable quality requirement.

Read explanation

A label merely assigned to an object is outside this technical sense. A characteristic may be specified or assessed without yet knowing whether the object fulfills the requirement.

Keep the characteristic distinct from its observed value, an indicator used to track it, and the judgment made from the evidence.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

operational fulfillment of quality requirements

Quality control

Quality control is operational work used to keep processes or outputs aligned with applicable quality requirements.

Read explanation

It can include checking results, finding departures, and adjusting or correcting the work. The applicable requirements and the scope of the check determine what a passing result establishes.

Some control activities also provide evidence for quality assurance. An inspection is one possible control activity, not the whole of quality control.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

appraisal of an object’s quality

Quality evaluation

A quality evaluation is a reasoned appraisal of how well a specified object meets relevant quality requirements or criteria.

Read explanation

It identifies the object, the basis for judging quality, the evidence considered, and the conclusion reached. The conclusion may be qualified when evidence is limited or criteria point in different directions.

Quality evaluation and quality assessment overlap in some fields. When a distinction is useful, evaluation emphasizes the warranted appraisal, while assessment emphasizes the work of obtaining and interpreting evidence.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

translating investigated customer needs into development decisions

Quality function deployment

Quality function deployment (QFD) is a structured method for translating investigated customer needs into product or service design and subsequent development decisions across organizational functions.

Read explanation

It makes proposed links between what customers need and what a team plans to design, build, and control. The links and priorities should be reasoned through rather than treated as facts merely because they occupy cells in a chart.

The House of Quality is a familiar planning matrix within QFD. QFD can continue into further design and process decisions; completing one matrix does not show that the finished offering meets customer needs.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

deliberate beneficial change in quality

Quality improvement

Quality improvement is deliberate change aimed at making an object, process, or system better at meeting relevant quality needs or requirements.

Read explanation

An improvement effort should identify what is to change, the desired result, and evidence that could show whether the change helped. A revised procedure alone does not show that performance improved.

Improvement can address recurring failures, strengthen a process, or add capabilities that meet changing needs. Evidence of the resulting effect distinguishes an achieved improvement from an intended one.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

quality assessment across domains

Quality indicator

A quality indicator is a specified sign used to gather evidence about one aspect of quality.

Read explanation

It can describe a feature of an object, how a service is provided, or a result experienced by its users.

Its interpretation depends on the quality question, the population and setting, the data, and the relationship between the sign and the aspect being judged.

In health care, quality indicators may concern structure, process, or outcome.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

cross-domain evaluative conclusion

Quality judgment

A quality judgment is a reasoned conclusion about how well an object meets relevant requirements or evaluative criteria.

Read explanation

It identifies the object and the basis for judging it, interprets pertinent evidence, and states the resulting appraisal.

The conclusion may be qualified when evidence is incomplete or criteria conflict.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

organizational direction and operation concerning quality

Quality management

Quality management is the coordinated work by which an organization sets quality aims and directs its activities toward meeting and improving them.

Read explanation

It connects policies and objectives with responsibilities, processes, resources, and evidence about results. Planning, assurance, control, and improvement are related functions within this work.

Managing quality does not itself mean that every output is good. Its effectiveness must be judged by relevant results and needs.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

organizational system for managing quality

Quality management system

A quality management system is the connected organizational arrangement through which quality objectives are set, pursued, checked, and improved.

Read explanation

It joins roles, processes, resources, information, and review across the organization. Documents and software may support the system, but neither alone constitutes it.

The system’s value depends on whether those connected activities help meet quality objectives; a collection of documents alone is not sufficient.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

specified quality assessment across services

Quality measure

A quality measure specifies how to obtain evidence about one aspect of quality.

Read explanation

It identifies what is assessed, the eligible population or events, the data and calculation or observation method, and the direction in which results should be interpreted.

In health care, quality measures may address care processes, outcomes, patient perceptions, or organizational structures.

In broader quality management, the aspect assessed should be tied to relevant characteristics and requirements.

For other services, the specification must fit the service, its users, and the decision it will inform.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

cross-domain quantitative quality metric

Quality metric

A quality metric specifies how to turn observations into a numerical assessment of one aspect of quality.

Read explanation

Its specification identifies the subject, relevant characteristic, data, calculation, unit or scale, and conditions of use.

The calculated value is a result; a requirement, target, or other interpretive rule is needed to decide what that result means.

The relevant quality aspect, calculation, and interpretation depend on the field; a numerical result has no universal grade on its own.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

organized representation of quality aspects

Quality model

A quality model is an explicit scheme for organizing the aspects of quality considered for a class of objects.

Read explanation

It may define characteristics, narrower subcharacteristics, and their relationships to measures. Its categories and boundaries depend on the objects and use for which it was made.

A model helps select what to examine; it does not itself set a requirement or establish that a particular object is good. Software and systems quality models are one domain example.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

arrangements for quality objectives

Quality planning

Quality planning is the work of setting quality objectives and arranging what is needed to achieve them.

Read explanation

The arrangements may identify applicable requirements, processes, responsibilities, resources, checks, and responses to foreseeable failure. A quality plan records such arrangements for a particular product, service, or project.

Planning specifies intended work and criteria; later control, assurance, and evaluation show what was done and what the evidence supports.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

structured multi-aspect representation

Quality profile

A quality profile is a structured view of how an object fares across specified aspects of quality.

Read explanation

It identifies the object and the aspects represented, with observations, assessments, or values for each. A profile can preserve differences that a single overall score would conceal.

In data and information quality, profiles can be computed at the level of an item, flow, process, or organization. Extending the term beyond that domain requires a stated framework and evidence.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

quality management

Quality requirement

A quality requirement is a need or expectation concerning an object’s quality that is to be fulfilled.

Read explanation

It may concern a characteristic, a level of performance, or the conditions under which the object is used. Like other requirements, it can be stated, generally implied, or obligatory.

Identify the object, the applicable condition, and the basis for deciding fulfillment. The requirement is the expectation; an observed result is evidence about whether it has been met.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

customer-to-technical grid in a House of Quality

Relationship matrix

In a House of Quality, the relationship matrix is the grid that pairs customer requirements with proposed technical characteristics and provides a cell for each assessed link.

Read explanation

The rows represent customer-side needs and the columns represent design-side responses. A mark or score expresses an assessed relationship under a stated convention; different methods may use different scales.

The grid helps a team consider which responses address which needs. Its entries are planning judgments and should not be mistaken for measurements of the finished product or service.

test-score consistency and engineered-item performance

Reliability

Reliability concerns whether a specified result or required performance holds consistently under the conditions that matter.

Read explanation

Its technical meaning depends on what is expected to remain dependable:

For test scores, reliability or precision concerns consistency across appropriate repetitions of the measurement procedure. The repetitions, population, sources of error, and intended score use must be specified.

For an engineered item or system, reliability concerns performing as required without failure over a specified interval and under stated conditions. When modeled probabilistically, R(t) is the probability that the item survives beyond time t.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

quality management and technical specifications

Requirement

Requirement is a need or expectation used to specify what an object, process, or action is expected to satisfy.

Read explanation

In quality management, it may be stated, generally implied by established practice, or obligatory; it need not be written down to apply.

To judge whether a requirement is fulfilled, identify what is expected, of what, and under which conditions.

In a technical specification, state it clearly enough to check fulfillment without prescribing a particular solution unless that solution is itself required.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

documented technical requirements

Specification

A specification records requirements for an object, process, or service in a form intended to guide its creation or assessment.

Read explanation

It may describe characteristics, limits, methods, or acceptance criteria. To support consistent decisions, its applicable requirements need to be clear enough to check.

A specification states what is expected; test results or other observations show whether the expectation was met. Conforming to it does not establish that it captured every relevant need.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evaluative reference; documentary and measurement uses distinguished

Standard

In an evaluative use, a standard is a reference or expected level used to judge performance or acceptability in a stated context.

Read explanation

A criterion identifies a consideration for judgment, while a standard can specify the level or condition expected for it; the terms overlap in some fields.

An indicator supplies an observable or assessable sign relevant to the judgment, but its result alone does not establish that the chosen standard is appropriate.

A standard can be communicated through descriptions, exemplars, or numerical limits; a defensible judgment names whose standard applies and what decision it governs.

The word also has other uses: a documentary standard may specify terminology, procedures, tests, or requirements, while a measurement standard is a reference for quantity values.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

results monitoring and evaluation

Target

A target states the result sought on a specified indicator, for a specified population or scope, by a specified time.

Read explanation

It helps people plan and later assess progress.

Its value should be justified by the objective, starting conditions, context, and resources; the word target supplies no universal cutoff.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

design-side response in a House of Quality

Technical characteristic

In a House of Quality, a technical characteristic is a property of the proposed product, service, or process that a team can specify or influence in response to customer requirements.

Read explanation

It names a design-side “how” and should be observable or measurable enough to guide development. The customer requirement names the need-side “what.”

The characteristic is distinct from its technical target, which states a desired level. A matrix cell records an assessed link between the characteristic and a customer requirement, not proof of achieved performance.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

desired level for a technical characteristic in a House of Quality

Technical target

In a House of Quality, a technical target is a specified desired value, range, or level for a technical characteristic in a proposed design.

Read explanation

It answers “how much” of that characteristic the design should achieve. A useful target names its measure or scale, units where applicable, and the relevant conditions.

Customer needs, feasibility, and comparisons may inform the choice of target. The target is a design aim; a later measurement is needed to determine whether the delivered offering reaches it.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

decision and classification boundary

Threshold

A threshold separates regions to which different interpretations or rules apply.

Read explanation

Its meaning is incomplete until the relevant variable, direction of comparison, boundary value, and consequence of crossing it are specified.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

limits on knowledge or prediction

Uncertainty

Uncertainty is a limit on what can be known or predicted about a specified state, claim, quantity, or outcome.

Read explanation

It can arise from incomplete observations, variability, assumptions, or limits in a model. State what is uncertain and why; uncertainty about a measurement differs from uncertainty about the consequences of a decision.

A probability or range can express some uncertainties, but neither is always available or sufficient. The limits should travel with the claim or recommendation they affect.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evidence-based confirmation for intended use

Validation

Validation is confirmation, using objective evidence, that requirements for a specified intended use or application have been fulfilled.

Read explanation

It asks whether the object is suitable for that use under the conditions being considered. The intended use, users, and relevant conditions should therefore be explicit.

A system can meet its written specification and still fail validation if that specification misses what the intended use requires. Verification and validation need not have the same result.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

deductive logic, test-score interpretation, and empirical research

Validity

Validity is the status or degree of warrant for a specified inference, interpretation, or use, judged by the rules relevant to that claim.

Read explanation

The word has distinct technical senses:

In deductive logic, an argument is valid when no possible interpretation makes all its premises true and its conclusion false. This is a yes-or-no property of the inference.

In measurement, validity concerns how well evidence and theory support a particular interpretation of scores for a stated use. Calling a test simply “valid” leaves out the interpretation and use that need justification.

In empirical research, validity concerns whether conclusions are warranted by the study: for example, whether an observed effect can be attributed to an intervention and whether the conclusion applies in the intended setting.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evaluative sense

Value

In its evaluative sense, value is the significance something has under an account of what merits concern, appreciation, use, or pursuit.

Read explanation

A useful value claim says what matters, to whom, in what setting, and why.

Evaluation research distinguishes factual description from a judgment that brings evidence and relevant values together.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

evidence-based confirmation of specified requirements

Verification

Verification is confirmation, using objective evidence, that specified requirements have been fulfilled.

Read explanation

It asks whether the object or result meets the requirements being checked. The method and evidence must fit those requirements.

Passing verification does not alone show that the requirements suit the intended use. That further question belongs to validation.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

customer evidence used in QFD inquiry

Voice of the customer

In QFD, voice of the customer is information gathered about identified customers’ needs, expectations, and use of a product or service.

Read explanation

It may include what customers say directly and what investigation reveals about needs they have not stated. Who was heard, in what setting, and through which method affects what the information can support.

A customer statement is evidence to interpret, not automatically a well-specified requirement. A team should test its translation of that information before using it to direct a design.

CC0 1.0View versioned object

argument-analysis link between grounds and claim

Warrant

In argument analysis, a warrant is the principle that explains why stated grounds support a claim.

Read explanation

The grounds supply case-specific information; the warrant supplies the linking reason. It may be stated or left implicit, and making it explicit helps readers test whether the move to the claim is sound.

broad evaluative and practical-decision use

Worth

In its broad evaluative use, worth is the value, merit, or importance attributed to something on stated grounds.

Read explanation

In a practical decision, “Is it worth it?” asks whether the reasons to pursue or obtain something justify its effort, cost, risk, and forgone alternatives.

That question may call for judgment without yielding a single monetary answer.
